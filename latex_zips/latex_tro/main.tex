\begin{abstract}
In this paper, we present a knowledge-based decision-making framework that enables robots to selectively request human feedback under uncertainty, addressing when to ask and what to ask. We formulate the problem as a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP) over a STRIPS-style symbolic knowledge base, where the robot maintains multiple world hypotheses and requests state-level feedback only when they cannot be reliably distinguished. Otherwise, it continues autonomous planning and execution. The framework is implemented in a full system architecture integrating sensing, planning, feedback management, and user interaction. We validate the approach in two robotic domains, tomato harvesting and waste sorting, through system experiments and a Wizard-of-Oz user study. Results show that our state-level querying strategy resolves task-relevant ambiguity with fewer interactions than existing policies, while maintaining comparable or higher task success and reducing perceived workload.
\end{abstract}
