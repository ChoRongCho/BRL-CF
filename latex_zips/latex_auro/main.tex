\abstract{
In this paper, we consider planning under partial observability, where a robot's own observations may be insufficient to disambiguate among competing hypotheses about the world state. Continuing execution under such unresolved ambiguity can lead the planner to commit to an incorrect hypothesis. However, resolving every such ambiguity through external queries would incur unnecessary information-acquisition costs. The key challenge is therefore to determine both when a query is necessary and what information should be requested. We propose a knowledge-based planning framework that represents alternative world states as a weighted belief over symbolic hypotheses. After each action and observation, the framework computes an entropy-based confidence measure and determines whether the belief is sufficiently resolved according to a predefined threshold. If additional information is required, it selects the grounded symbolic fact that minimizes the expected posterior entropy and queries the external expert for its Boolean truth value. The answer eliminates inconsistent hypotheses, updates the knowledge base, and enables planning to continue from the refined belief. We evaluate the framework in two robotic task domains with different uncertainty structures and compare it with alternative query-timing and query-selection strategies. The results demonstrate that determining both when and what to query improves task success while limiting unnecessary external information acquisition.
}
